# Decisions for the 2026-10-03 derivation fixes

These are your calls on findings from `2026-10-03-derivation-audit.md`, in the order you made them. The fix plans quote them. The triage table `2026-10-03-fix-triage.md` lists which findings still need a call.

## Step 1: engine facts

**D1 (finding 28), 2026-10-03.** When the SP cap clamps a window, the transfer scale is measured from the collecting note (the stored `clamp_tick`), not the activation. Why: the collecting note is the one that moves the SP end, as the Rolling in the Deep FC video proved. The engine already stores it (ADR 0013). This changes stored scales, so it needs a results-stamp bump. Finding 27's fix (the pre-SqIn SP end) is in the same function, so both go in together under one bump.

**D2 (finding 32), 2026-10-03.** The replay keeps its own chord-by-chord walk as a cross-check on the engine's totals. For "did Star Power pay this chord?" it asks the shared rule in `core/backend_value.h` instead of deciding it itself. The Preview's xN disc doubles only when SP actually paid something for the chord. So a squeezed-out chord that SP pays nothing shows the plain multiplier, and a partly paid one still shows doubled. No stored change.

**D3 (findings 90 and 89), 2026-10-03.** A tied variant folded into its leader while SP is still running takes the leader's closed activation from the fold point: its SP end, clamp note, collected phrases and backend rows. It keeps its own activation note. Why: from the fold point their futures are identical, so the leader's closed activation is the variant's real one. The search does not change. Finding 89 is fixed alongside: each variant stores its own leftover SP, and which paths are listed stays as it is. Results-stamp bump, shared with D1.

**D4 (finding 332), 2026-10-03.** The record may hold an explicit "unknown" transfer scale instead of a silent x1.00, and the details view then shows a short "transfer scale unknown" note and no eff. figures. But your condition is that this must never happen: "there should never be a situation when the scale cant be computed". So the plan must close every way of reaching it at the source: every activation gets a stored SP end, and a zero or negative measure length can't reach the engine (this ties to R7.6, negative `.chart` tempos). A test must prove that no fresh record stores "unknown". The "unknown" value is a guard that shows a bug, not an expected state.

**D5 (R7.10, with finding 30), 2026-10-03.** For an activation whose SP outlasts the song, the 500 ms backend window is measured from the SP end, the same as every other activation. Why: only notes that early activation plus a late hit could push out of SP matter, and those sit well inside 500 ms of the SP end. The engine's tail collection and the display share one "inside the squeeze window" check next to `kSqueezeWindowMs`. Nothing on screen or in stored records changes, because the display already filtered by this rule before saving.

**D6 (R7.6, finding 100; step-1 plan Q1), 2026-10-03.** Timing that makes a measure or beat last zero, negative or infinite time refuses the chart with a plain error naming the tick. A time signature with a top number of 0 is ignored in both formats, as `.chart` already does. This closes one of D4's sources.

**D112 (drum lanes in the Preview), 2026-10-10.** The user asked for the Preview to show drum lanes: the strips Clone Hero draws down one or two pad columns during a roll or trill. A scout read how the game builds them in Clone Hero 1.1.0.6142 (its listings are in `docs/audit/drum-lanes/`), and the main session checked the .mid facts against `testdata`. Onyx and the game disagree on which pads light; in `testdata`, 4 of 50 lanes differ, all single lanes that start on a chord (for example *Victoria – Grow* at tick 29856, a red roll that opens on a blue crash). The user chose:
1. **Onyx's look.** Each lit pad gets Onyx's lane strip at full strength, drawn before the fill strips, as Onyx's `drawLanes` does (ADR 0008).
2. **The game's rules for what a lane is.** A .chart lane is special phrase `S 65` (single) or `S 66` (double) in a difficulty's section, and shows on that difficulty. A .mid lane is pitch 126 (single lane marker) or pitch 127 (double lane marker) on the drum track, note-on to note-off. Its note-on velocity names the lowest difficulty that shows it, and every higher difficulty shows it too: velocity 21 to 30 is Easy, velocity 31 to 40 is Medium, velocity 41 to 50 is Hard, and any other velocity is Expert.
3. **The game's rule for which pads light.** Kicks never count. The first chord with a non-kick note, from one tick before the lane to its end, decides. A single lane lights that chord's lowest pad in the game's order: red, yellow tom, blue tom, green tom, yellow cymbal, blue cymbal, green cymbal. A double lane adds the highest pad of a second chord: the first chord itself when it has two or more pads, otherwise the next chord with a non-kick note. A lane with no such chord is dropped. The pads are picked before Note Shuffle runs, as in the game. The rule's one home is `light_drum_lanes` in `src/parse/song.cpp`.
4. **Any note inside the lane counts.** The game drops a lane whose first note lands more than a small tolerance after its start. That tolerance is resolution × 12 divided by a value the game sets at runtime, and nobody has read that value. The user chose not to guess it. All 50 lanes in `testdata` start exactly on their first note.

The game also reads lanes during play, to make the notes under a lane easier to hit. Hydra's engine does not, and this decision changes no score.

*Correction, 2026-10-17.* Item 3's sentence "The pads are picked before Note Shuffle runs, as in the game" was wrong. Hydra now picks a lane's pads after Note Shuffle, from the shuffled notes. The rule for which chords decide is unchanged. Kicks never move and the shuffle removes no note, so the same chords decide either way; only their colours change.

The user found it in the Preview. In *Glide* by Between the Buried and Me, around 1:51, a red lane strip sat over yellow cymbals and blue toms with Note Shuffle on.

The listings show why. The game does build its lanes before its shuffle: the chart builder in `docs/audit/note-shuffle/step0/open2/f20d2ea0_build.asm` calls the lane builder at 0x20D317E and the shuffle at 0x20D3315. But a lane does not store a colour. The lane builder in `docs/audit/drum-lanes/f20d12c0.asm` keeps a reference to the lane's first chord (saved to rsp+0x70 at 0x20D1585) and, for a double lane, to the second chord (saved to rsp+0x38 at 0x20D15FB; a single lane stores null there at 0x20D1625). It writes those references into the lane at +0x20 (0x20D17AA) and +0x28 (0x20D17BC). It reads each chord's lane mask, the 16-bit word at +0x80, through a note it reaches from that chord (0x20D15BA and 0x20D1619). The shuffle in `docs/audit/note-shuffle/step0/open2/f20f0c30_shuffle.asm` rewrites that same word in place on the notes it walks (0x20F1151). So the lane holds notes, and the shuffle recolours them after the lane is built.

The routine that draws a lane was not read. The claim that the drawn strip follows the recoloured notes rests on the fields above and on the user's Glide report. The rule's one home is still `light_drum_lanes`, now called once from the loader tail after the shuffle switch (`finish_loaded_song` in `src/parse/song.cpp`). D254 later changed which pads a lane lights while Note Shuffle is on; with it off, item 3 stands.

In a .mid the EVENTS handler (0x21544E0) takes `[end]` with its brackets. In a .chart the [Events] reader (0x2163170) strips one pair of brackets, so `end` and `[end]` both count; the flag that skips this (`settings+0x2C`) is 0 in ordinary play, because the load coroutine calls `FUN_18035c110(song, 0, 0, 1, 0)`. The first end event in the file wins (setter 0x212E310). `[music_end]` is not among the game's strings, and song.ini `song_length` is never read on this path; the game writes the audio's length into it when it is missing (0x358850), which is why it usually equals the audio length. The main session checked the shape of the length lines and the `FUN_18035c110` call in the decompiled coroutine; the rest is the scouts' reading. A .sng or .srb holds a whole `notes.mid` or `notes.chart`, and Hydra hands it to the same two parsers, so the end event reaches all four formats.

Hydra never opens audio for a length (D75 item 5), so something had to stand in for the audio length. The user's calls, picked from the plan's options:
1. **What stands in for the audio length.** "Option C (Recommended)". With a stated length, the length is the earlier of the end event and the stated length. With no stated length, or one dropped because it ends before the last note, it is the end event if there is one, else the last note. The stated length, moved into chart time by the delay or Offset as D75 item 3 says, sits in the audio's seat. It matches the game whenever the stated length is the game's own record of the audio.
2. **The one-second pad.** "Copy the pad (Recommended)". The length is the call 1 answer plus 1,000 ms, as in the game, so the song ends in Hydra where it ends in Clone Hero.
3. **The last note.** "All instruments". The last note is the latest note end (start plus sustain) in any instrument section at any difficulty, as in Clone Hero. This is the wider parse, not the plan's default, so Hydra keeps no departure here. The check that drops a stated length compares against this note, and the end event's one-beat margin counts from this note's tick. The cost is a wider read on each click that works out a length: the .mid reader keeps note messages on every instrument track, and the .chart reader every section's notes.
4. **The one-beat margin.** "Copy the margin (Recommended)". An end event counts when its tick is at least the last note end's tick plus `Resolution` ticks. Equal counts; one tick less does not. Hydra works at 100% speed, so the beat is the chart's `Resolution` with no new number.
5. **One end everywhere.** Asked later the same day, the user wrote: "the problem is that we have two different rules. there should one rule that every function uses. we already know how clone hero decides the end of the song, we don't need different places deriving different rules." Asked what the Preview does with the audio length it measures, the user chose "Ignore it (Recommended)". So `app::song_length_ms` is the one end of a song. The Preview's playback stops there, and the time box's total, the scrubber's edge and the gold marks end there too (`PreviewTransport::length_ms`). The measured audio end is never an input. With no length at all (no stated length, no end event, no notes), nothing stops the Preview's clock. This replaces D48's playback range (Q25): playback no longer runs to the later of the last note and the audio's end, and audio past the song's end no longer plays.

Worked results under these calls, from the plan's table: Allister - Overrated (end event 150,098 ms before its stated 151,394 ms) becomes 151,098 ms; Thrice - Deadbolt (end event 186,359 ms after its stated 185,087 ms) becomes 186,087 ms; Karnivool - Themata (no end event) becomes 342,971 ms; Rime, whose stated length is dropped, is expected at 816,463 ms (13:36.5), the game's own number, if its end event clears the margin over all instruments. Chaotrope - Senescence (shortened), in `testdata/input/common/Summer Blast _25 Setlist/Tier 5`, has another instrument section running to 314,996 ms, past its stated 277,000 ms and past its last drum note at 270,896 ms, and no end event. So its stated length is dropped and its length becomes 315,996 ms.

This replaces D75 item 1 (the length is the metadata's stated length) and item 2 (the backup is the start of the last Expert drum note) with the rule above. D75 items 3, 4 and 5 stand: the stated length is moved into chart time, one length drives the Paths timeline, the scrub bar and the SP meter, and no audio is opened. Call 5 makes the same length the Preview's playback end. Nothing stored changes, because no length is stored (`store::SongLength`), so no stamp moves. One known difference stays: where a charter's stated length differs from the real audio, Hydra still uses the stated length in the audio's seat.

**D256 (session e5fc6cff), 2026-10-21. A transfer scale less than a thousandth from 1 counts as no scale.** This replaces D14's 1e-9 tolerance. The transfer scale is the ratio of the bar lengths at the SP end and at the activation (`transfer_scale_between` in `src/core/squeeze_rating.cpp`). D14 counted a scale as no scale only within 1e-9 of 1, on the premise that no real multiplier is that close to 1. MIDI files break that premise. They store each tempo in whole microseconds per quarter note, so two bars meant to be the same length can differ by a few parts per million. Tapestry of the Starless Abstract, a chart in the user's library and not in `testdata/input`, shows it. The main session read its tempo map in this session: it switches from 140 BPM in 4/4 (428,571 µs per quarter) to 105 BPM in 6/8 (571,429 µs per quarter) at tick 433,440. A 4/4 bar there lasts 4 x 428,571 = 1,714,284 µs and a 6/8 bar 3 x 571,429 = 1,714,287 µs, so the late scale is 1,714,287 / 1,714,284 = 1.00000175. The Paths tab showed an orange "Frontend timing scales x1.000002 (late) at the SP end." and "(eff. 71.4 ms)" tags that changed nothing a player could see. The user's call in chat: "Count a scale as “no scale” when the difference from 1 is less than .001".

So `is_scaled` in `src/core/squeeze_rating.h` is true exactly when the scale is 0.001 or more away from 1, and `kScaleIdentityTolerance` is 1e-3. A difference of exactly 0.001 counts as scaled; the user confirmed in chat: "1.001 is not a difference of less than .001". The stored double of 1.001 sits a hair under that distance, so `is_scaled` allows for one machine epsilon of storage error (`kScaleStorageAllowance`). The scale line's digit cap, `kScaleIdentityDigits`, follows from the tolerance and is now 3, so a scaled multiplier still never prints as x1.000. `is_scaled` is display-only: it decides a row's eff. figure (`NoteRating::effective_ms`) and the orange flag (`scale_governs`). The search and the stored records never read it, and stored scales keep full precision, so no stamp moves.

**D257 (session ea3cb421), 2026-10-22. A collapsed activation row shows a badge for every timing it has.** This extends D80. The user reported that on "The Age of Betrayal" (Halcyon Way), optimal path `E3+ E2+ 2 2 3`, rows 1 and 2 showed only `early fill 0 ms`, although their notation has a `+` (a squeeze-in). The plan is `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-22-squeeze-badge-beside-early-fill.md`. That `+` is a free squeeze: a squeeze-in at or before the SP end, figure 0 or below. D80 lets a free squeeze onto the row only "when nothing on the activation needs timing", and the zero-slack early fill counts as a timing, so D80 hid the squeeze. The squeeze showed only in the open row. Nobody had ruled "one badge per row"; the one-badge shape came from the 2.0 design.

The user's calls, in chat on 2026-10-22:
1. **A free squeeze beside an early fill.** "Yes, show both". A free squeeze keeps its badge even when the row also has an early fill badge, so the user's rows read `early fill 0 ms` and then `squeeze in -X ms`, both grey. X is the same whole number Copy path prints for that activation.
2. **How many badges.** "One per timing". A row gets one badge per timing it has, in the notation's order: the early fill first, then each squeeze in list order. That is the order Copy path and the Preview already print the figures in. A row holds at most three badges. Each badge is orange by the rule its own line in the open row already uses, so a free squeeze is never orange, and the timeline mark is orange when any of its row's badges is.
3. **Narrow panels.** "Drop to a second line". The badges stay flush right on the row's line when they fit beside the bars. When they would cross the bars, every badge moves below the row's line, inside the same row. No width or wording changes. Call 4 says where they go there.
4. **Where dropped badges go.** "Under the measure". The user saw the first build of call 3 in real Hydra at a narrow panel and said it "looks ugly as hell": the dropped pills touched the first line's text and stayed flush right, lining up with nothing. Now dropped badges start at the measure's column and run left to right in badge order, one pill gap apart, as on the row's line. The gap above them, between their lines and below them is the row's own top padding, the space between the row's top and its first line's text. A badge that would end further right than a flush-right badge starts the next line under the measure, so three of the longest badge wrap at the narrowest panel. Rows whose badges fit beside the bars stay as they were: flush right, one line tall. The rule is `activation_row_layout` in `src/ui/activation_row_layout.h`.
5. **One font on the row.** "All mono". The bars used to be drawn in the main font and the measure in the mono font, from the same top, so the bars sat a few pixels lower than the measure on every row. Now every piece of a row's text is in the mono font: the number in its circle, the notation, the measure, the bars and every badge. The layout measures the bars and the badges in that font too. Mono badges are wider, so at the narrowest panel one longest badge beside an 11-character measure and `12 bars` now goes under the measure too.

What stays. D80's example still holds: an activation whose only timing is a free squeeze-in 316 ms inside Star Power reads exactly `squeeze in -316 ms`. D51 call 4 and D60 item 2 stay: an early fill with time to spare has nothing to time and gets no badge. Everything else D80 kept on `hardest()` stays there, with its tie rule: the path button, the Path limit, the library filter, the stored hardest ms, the report and the all-0 list still treat a free squeeze as nothing to time. Nothing stored changes, so no stamp moves.
