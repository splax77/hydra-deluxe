**Why so much model time (the coordinator's question).** Four drivers, all visible in the drafters:

- Context size. The four drafters averaged 194k to 247k tokens of context per call and peaked at 353k to 430k. With 105 to 138 calls each, every call pays a 200k-token prefill before the first output token. That is the "model-wait" bucket: 15 to 20 minutes per drafter.
- Cold reading, in pieces. Each drafter read 22 to 35 distinct source files, mostly the same ones: `engine.cpp`, `graph.cpp`, `model.h`, `path_codec.cpp`, `preview_view.cpp` were each opened by 6 or 7 agents across the two sessions, and the 7,002-line audit report was opened by 18 agents. Reads come in chunks, so 48 to 59 Read calls per drafter, each one growing the context.
- Long outputs, written twice. Each drafter wrote a 700 to 1,340-line draft, then a full second version after the integration rulings. 73k to 154k characters written per drafter; 4.5 to 9.5 minutes per drafter in the "model-gen" bucket. The final plan is 4,641 lines, 297 KB, because it carries full code listings for every task.
- Thinking per step. 49 to 78 thinking blocks per drafter (content is not stored, so the size is unknown). Each block sits in front of a tool call, so the per-call cost is thinking plus prefill, not just prefill.

**Parked time: all of it was waiting on the coordinator, none on the user.** In A the four drafters finished between 21:21 and 21:28 and the coordinator sent the revision messages at 21:30, after reading all four and writing the rulings. Parked total 22 minutes of agent time (8.7 + 5.5 + 5.3 + 2.6). In B the Task 3 implementer was parked 5.8 min while its review ran and the coordinator wrote the fix brief. The user was never the one being waited on by an agent; the user's own waits are in section 4.

## 2. Which time was avoidable

**The revision round of the plan drafts (about 30 agent-minutes of rewriting plus 22 parked, 10 minutes of wall clock).** Parts A and D both designed the stored SP-end history, because the brief split the plan by finding group, not by data structure. The coordinator's thinking at 21:29 says it plainly: "each draft individually looks fine, but combined they violate the derive-once rule by storing duplicate facts." If the record shape had been decided before the fan-out (one short design pass, or one drafter owning the record first), the other three could have drafted against it once.

**Full code listings in the plan (unknown wall clock here, but it drove the review rounds in section 3).** Drafters spent most of their output on complete C++ test files. Those listings carried the copies the reviews later found. Writing signatures, test names and expected numbers instead would cut the drafters' writing time by half or more and shrink every later reader's context.

**Re-reading the audit report (18 agents).** The triage JSON (`docs/audit/2026-10-03-fix-triage.json`) already carries each finding's one-line summary and owner. Agents still opened the 7,002-line report in chunks. Giving each agent the exact finding texts it owns, in its brief, would remove most of those reads.

**Serial decision calls (3 to 4 minutes of the user's attention).** The five step-1 calls were asked one at a time between 20:57 and 21:02, with 22 to 30 s of model work between each answer and the next question. Two calls of up to four questions would have done it.

**Serial Task 1 then Task 2 in the gate plan (about 5 minutes).** They touched different files; the transcript's reason was "share one branch". The parallel-by-default rule was written later that day, so this is the rule arriving, not failing.

**Hook denials: 24 across the slice, each costing seconds.** `sed` blocked (A main, twice), Windows-path listing in Bash (A main, twice), the attribution gate firing on quoted "git commit" text in a ledger append (B main, 20:21, which spawned a 3.6 min fix agent). Small.

## 3. Review rounds, and what the plans could have prevented

The review rounds ran in session a9a461da, not in this slice. I read its verdict blocks (`audit/findings_a9a.txt`) to see which findings a better plan would have stopped.

**Step 1: 7 rounds, 15:33 to 17:34 on 2026-10-04 (121 minutes), 31 findings.** Round 1 found 14, then 6, 4, 4, 2, 1, CLEAN. **Step 2: 4 rounds, 17:48 to 18:36 (48 minutes), 12 findings** (9, 2, 1, CLEAN). **T10 and T11: 3 rounds, 18:46 to 19:18 (32 minutes), 8 findings** (7, 1, CLEAN). Fifty-one findings in all.

About two thirds of them are test-side, and most of those trace straight to the plan text:

- **Tests that recompute the answer instead of pinning a number.** Step 1 round 1 findings 8, 9, 10; step 2 round 1 finding 7 (four tests); T10 round 1 finding 1 (seven offsets written as `song.timecode(A).ms() - song.timecode(B).ms()`). The step-1 plan states the rule in its Global Constraints ("Test fixtures state literal ticks rather than recomputing engine values") and then breaks it in its own listings: `timecode(` appears 15 times, `plusmeasure` 9 times and `500.0` 4 times in the plan's code. The implementers copied what the plan gave them. A stated rule with a counter-example in the same document does not hold.
- **Test helpers copied between files.** Step 1: `windows_text` copied word for word, three ways to answer "does a tied variant price as it does alone", `collect_tied` copied as `collect_variants` twice, `check_one_step_per_sqin` written three times, two leftover "tied variants of every root" loops. Step 2: MIDI delta encoders and file builders in three new test files (audit finding 286 had already asked for `tests/midi_util.h` to own them), the `.srb` builder copied a fourth time, the leaderboard fixture copied, the `.chart` header text written in about eight places, the difficulty table pinned twice. T10: `deact_edge_at` copied, `keep_losers` duplicating `wide_search`, hand-written path walks where `flatten_paths` existed, activation tick lists rebuilt twice. The step-2 plan's Global Constraints made this worse by design: "Each task writes its new tests in its own new file, `tests/test_s2_<topic>.cpp`", with full listings per file. The step-2 plan mentions `[Song]` 13 times, `push_str` 3 times, `deflate_raw` twice, `fill_store` 3 times: the copies were in the plan. Neither plan has a task that owns a shared test-helper library; step 1 has `record_fixtures.h` for one task's fixtures and nothing for path walks, offsets, chart text or MIDI builders.
- **Numbers in tests with no decision.** Step 1 round 1 findings 6, 13, 14; round 2 finding 6; round 5 finding 2; T10 round 1 finding 3. These became D40, D43 and three D43 addenda during review, each a user round trip. Neither plan has a "test limits" table.
- **Scans written outside the one scan file.** Step 1 round 1 finding 12 (three new source-scanning tests), round 2 finding 5; step 2 round 1 finding 8. Neither plan mentions `test_single_owner.cpp` at all (zero matches in both). The step-1 plan was written at 21:05 to 21:43 on 10-03; the scan test had been committed at 20:08 the same evening in session B, so the plan could have named it.
- **Production owners the plan named but tasks rebuilt anyway.** Step 1 round 1 findings 1 to 5 and 7: a second "is this phrase chord after the SP end" test in the graph, "which SqIn step is the n-th" counted in both `squeeze_end_step` and `close_folded_act`, the clamp looked up twice in `clamp_tick` and `end_anchor_tick`. The plan's Appendix B lists the accessors by name, but Task 5 (the history, drafted by D) and Task 17 (the fold, drafted by B) were written by different drafters whose code never met until the reviewer read the merged branch. The integration rulings reconciled accessor names, not bodies.
- **Decisions the plan did not foresee.** Step 2 round 1 finding 1 (unsorted `.chart` sections, became D47) and T10 round 1 finding 6 (the fold exception, D44 addendum) were real behaviour questions found in review. The plans said "scores do not change" and had no extreme-tempo or malformed-file fixtures to test that claim; the 2,000+ BPM fuzz charts were built during execution.

**The gate plan (session B) had a design defect that cost the rest of the day.** The Task 3 reviewer (a02bbd2d5d543fed5) found six bypasses of the gate and the coordinator's own note says "all plan-mandated". The plan was written by the main session alone in nine minutes with no second read. The fix round and re-review cost about 17 minutes that evening, and the next day's reviews judged the command-line gate "unfixable as the judge" and replaced it with a git-side hook (memory `derive-once-gate-2026-10`). A ten-minute adversarial read of the plan before execution would likely have reached the same conclusion.

**Decisions by count.** The decisions file now holds 47 numbered decisions and 8 addenda. ## 5. Speedups for the next phase

1. **Decide the stored record shape before fanning out drafters.** One short pass (the coordinator or one Opus agent, about 10 min) writes the field list and accessor names; every drafter gets it as input. Saves the revision round: about 30 agent-minutes of rewriting and 22 parked, 10 minutes of wall clock, on a plan the size of step 1. Enforce in the plan brief: "Part drafts may not add stored fields; the record shape is in `<file>`."
2. **No full code listings in plans.** Tasks carry signatures, file and function names, test case names and the pinned numbers. Saves roughly half of each drafter's writing (4.5 to 9.5 min each) and cuts the 297 KB plan to a quarter, which every execution and review agent then reads. More important, it removes the copies the plan seeded (section 3). Enforce with a plan-lint script run before AskUserQuestion: fail on any fenced block over 20 lines, on `timecode(` or `plusmeasure` or a bare `500` inside a test listing.
3. **One shared-test-helper task before the wave, and a scan row for each helper.** Path walks, activation tick lists, SP-end offsets, chart text and MIDI builders each get one owner in `tests/` before any task starts; implementer briefs name them. Of the 51 review findings, about 33 were test-side copies or recomputes. Halving the rounds saves roughly 60 of step 1's 121 minutes, 20 of step 2's 48 and 15 of T10's 32, plus the fix agents' time in the execution slice. Enforce by making the helper task a wave-0 dependency and by adding the reviewer's recurring finding kinds (already listed in memory `derive-once-gate-2026-10`) to the implementer brief, not only the reviewer's.
4. **A test-limits table in every plan.** Every floor, seed, deadline, depth and band a test uses is listed with its reason and shown to the user with the plan. Would have folded D40, D43 and its three addenda into the plan's decision round: five user round trips and the fix rounds behind them.
5. **Name `tests/test_single_owner.cpp` in every plan's Global Constraints** as the only scan file. Zero mentions in both plans produced four findings across three merges.
6. **Batch decision questions.** Up to four per call; the five serial calls at 20:57 to 21:02 should have been two. Saves 3 to 4 minutes of the user's attention per round and keeps the user from sitting through model work between questions. Enforce in the planning skill text: "collect every question first, then ask."
7. **Brief agents with finding text, not the audit file.** The triage JSON has every finding's summary; put the owned findings' full text into the brief. Removes most of the 18 agents' chunked reads of the 7,002-line report (unknown minutes, but 0.5 to 1.5 min of reads per agent and a large share of the 200k-token contexts).
8. **Review a plan that contains gate or hook code before executing it.** The gate plan went straight from a nine-minute solo write to implementation and its own code held six bypasses. One reviewer pass of the plan (about 5 min) before dispatch. Enforce by adding "plans whose Files list touches `~/.claude/hooks` need a reviewer verdict on the plan file" to the SDD workflow.
9. **Send rulings to drafters as they finish, not after all four.** Part A waited 8.7 min for rulings that only concerned its overlap with Part D. Small, about 10 agent-minutes, but free.
