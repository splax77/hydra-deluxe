## SP-14

- Unknown was: why run 1's first activation started from a meter of 2,879 and not 2,880, and why its game end sat 2.9 ms after Hydra's.
- Looked at: every Star Power watch CSV under `RUNS\2026-11-06`, `RUNS\2026-11-07` and `RUNS\2026-11-08` (13 files, found by the file name pattern `*sp_watch*.csv`), and the four in `tools/ch_probe/experiments/results/overlay-spike/` (run 1's own file `run1_cal0_sp_watch.csv`, and `run2_cal30`, `run2b_vid30`, `run2c_vid30`). The script `docs/audit/2026-11-08-clone-hero-unknowns/W11a/sp14_first_active_rows.py` finds each row where `sp_active_byte` goes from 0 to 1. It prints the meter on that row and on the row before it, and tests whether a short meter fits the game's own drain inside the gap between the two rows. Its output is `W11a/sp14_first_active_rows.tsv` (the dated run folders) and `W11a/sp14_overlay_spike_rows.tsv` (the 2026-10-29 runs). The 2.9 ms end figure was read in `overlay-spike/run1_cal0_sp_compare.txt`.
- Found: the meter was a whole number of quarter bars just before every activation, and the one-unit shortfall is drain the watcher sampled after the activation. Over the dated folders there are 52 activations in 8 of the 13 files (five files have none). The last row before each activation holds a whole number of quarters in all 52 (7,200 or 14,400 of 14,400, or 2,880, 4,320 or 5,760 of 5,760). The first active row is 1 to 4 units short in 17 of the 52. The 2026-10-29 runs show the same thing: `run1_cal0_sp_watch.csv` reads 2,880 at clock 30.3412 s, then 2,879 at 30.3472 s with the active byte set. Its other three activations read 2,880. `run2_cal30` repeats the 2,879 on its first activation, and `run2b_vid30` shows it on its second instead. Every short row fits drain: the watcher's rows are 3.03 to 6.13 ms apart in song clock, most often 6.06 (column `gap_ms`). The game drains the shortfall in 1.85 to 3.74 ms at the rate it shows over the next 50 rows (column `drain_ms`), and that is no longer than the gap in all 20 short rows across both tables. So the meter starts whole, and the watcher reads it a few milliseconds after the activation, when the drain has already taken a unit or more. Which activation shows it depends on where the sample falls. The 2.9 ms end gap is a separate question that these files do not settle. The game's end time (`sp_end_s`, 39.4883 s in run 1) is written at activation (it moves later only when a phrase lands during Star Power, as activation 4 in `run1_cal0_sp_compare.txt` shows). The rows bound the activation clock only to the 6.06 ms gap between 30.3412 and 30.3472 s, and the files hold no note time to compare it with.
- Proposed label: Proven in a live run, for a reworded rule: "The meter holds a whole number of quarter bars when Star Power turns on. A watcher row read a few milliseconds after the activation can show it 1 to 4 units short, because the drain has started (20 of 63 activations in the runs of 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-08)." The 2.9 ms gap between the game's end time and Hydra's stays Unknown, with this Evidence added: the watcher bounds the activation clock only to one 6 ms frame gap.
- Hydra: `activation_initial_end_times` in src/search/graph.h line 167 starts from whole bars. That agrees with the meter before activation in every run read, so no difference was found for the meter half.
