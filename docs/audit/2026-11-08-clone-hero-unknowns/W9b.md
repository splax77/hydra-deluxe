- Found: most of the block holds, with three corrections and two narrowings.

  How a press reaches the screen. Every menu's `Update` hands each player to the button method `0x1D4BC0` (`menu_update_1D7060.asm` lines 76 to 153, `menu_player_input_1D6B10.asm` line 16). That method asks Rewired for each action and calls one virtual slot per press. Green down calls slot 31 (`menu_buttons_1D4BC0.asm` lines 86 to 93 and 318 to 321), Red down slot 51 (lines 120 to 127 and 298 to 301). Yellow down calls slot 21 and Blue down slot 10 only when the profile's drums test is true (lines 128 to 135, 160 to 167, 268 to 296); otherwise they call slots 20 and 36, which `PlayerSelectionMenu` does not override and `BaseMenu` leaves empty (`dump.cs` lines 44886 and 44979, RVA 0x6D60). The drums test is true when the controller type is Drums (2) or FiveLaneDrums (3) and one global setting is 1 (`menu_drums_test_D7F20.asm` lines 9 to 23), or when the player has no profile (`menu_buttons_1D4BC0.asm` line 80). Which setting that is was not found. Two more actions, ids 5 and 14 (names not read), also call slots 10 and 21 for every controller type when Green is not held (lines 322 to 398). Their order swaps when the profile's instrument is Drums (6) or Pro Drums (9).

  Yellow and Blue. On the player screen, slot 10 and slot 21 do nothing on the Ready page and otherwise run the base down and up moves (`player_screen_down_32E710.asm` lines 9 to 13, `player_screen_up_330F30.asm` lines 9 to 13). Blue moves the highlight down and Yellow moves it up: the base moves add or take one from the cursor +0x40, or from the scroll +0x3C at the scrolling edge (`menu_cursor_down_1D57F0.asm` lines 34 and 36, `menu_cursor_up_1D6820.asm` lines 21 and 35).

  Green, page by page (`player_screen_green_32E190.asm`). Ready: nothing (lines 9 and 10). Confirm: the cursor alone picks the action; row 1 opens Instrument and clears +0x246, row 2 opens Difficulty, row 3 opens Modifiers, and row 0 saves the part to the profile and calls `0x32FF70`, which writes Ready to the page (lines 137 to 211). Instrument: `0x32FB60` sets the instrument from the highlighted row's text, cursor + scroll, by its name ("Drums" 6, "Pro Drums" 9, "No Part" 15 and ten more), saves it on the profile, and opens Difficulty when +0x1F0 is set, else returns to Confirm (`player_screen_instrument_32FB60.asm` lines 34 to 228). Difficulty: the row text at +0x218[cursor], the cursor alone, sets the difficulty by name, saves it on the profile, and goes to Modifiers when +0x1F1 is set, else Confirm; the row "Change Instrument" sets +0x1F0 and opens Instrument (lines 53 to 132). Modifiers: row 0 returns to Confirm, any other row toggles one modifier (lines 24 to 52).

  So three parts of the block are wrong. Green does not always move to the next page; it goes where the row or a flag says, and on Confirm it opens the page the row names. The instrument Green sets comes from the row's name string at +0x218, not from the instrument id array at +0x228; no read of +0x228 appears in any of these listings. And the highlighted row is cursor + scroll only on Instrument and Modifiers; Confirm and Difficulty read the cursor alone.

  The flags. +0x242 is exactly "the controller type is Drums or FiveLaneDrums" (`player_screen_instrument_32FB60.asm` lines 192 to 206), so the block's "is-drums" guess is proven. +0x1F0 is set by "Change Instrument" and decides whether the instrument pick goes on to Difficulty; it is cleared for "No Part". That makes it a "came here to change the instrument" flag, not a first-time flag. +0x246 stops Red from leaving the Instrument page (`player_screen_red_32CBE0.asm` lines 44 and 45) and is cleared when Confirm opens Instrument, so the block's "lock on going back" holds. +0x244 stops Red from leaving the screen from Confirm (`player_screen_red_32CBE0.asm` line 60); whether it is a network flag was not shown. A `CHNetManager` bool `0x145FE0` (not read) gates Red on Ready and Confirm and the save on Confirm row 0.

  Page values. 6 is written by `0x32D2D0` (`player_screen_page6_32D2D0.asm` line 5); 5 and 6 are passed to the second page setter `0x330350`; 7 is only compared. No write of 7 was found among the class's own methods; a write from outside the class was not searched for. So "the code sets the page to 5, 6 and 7" holds for 5 and 6 and is not shown for 7.
