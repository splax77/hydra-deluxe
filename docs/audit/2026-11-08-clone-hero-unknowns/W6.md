# W6: fills and phrases (CH-UNK-W6)

Workstream W6 of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-08-clone-hero-unknowns.md`. Each block was read whole on its page and judged from the game's code. Listings named "scratch" were made in the session's scratch folder and are not committed; every committed listing is under `docs/clone-hero/code/6142/`. Caller lists come from a byte scan of `GameAssembly.dll` for direct `call` and `jmp` instructions and RIP-relative loads, so they are "direct callers only (byte scan)": delegates, vtable slots and reflection were not searched unless a section says so.

## FL-08

- Unknown was: what the byte at a written fill's `+0x20` means, and what sets it.
- Looked at: new listings `docs/clone-hero/code/6142/fill_init_20FB9A0.asm`, `fill_skip_from_coda_215DA70.asm`, `fill_skip_before_215F610.asm` and `mid_events_coda_21544E0.asm`, each with its `.md`; `fill_to_activation_20CFF60.asm` lines 78 to 87; `mid_drum_reader_2155050.asm` lines 154 to 163; `docs/audit/drum-lanes/spec_213e0c0.asm` lines 101 to 114; `fill_cache_caller_20E49D0.asm` lines 85 to 99; `docs/audit/note-shuffle/step0/open2/f215c320.asm` lines 35 to 37; `C:\Users\Patrick\Downloads\Hydra\hydra-data\ch-dumps\6142\dump.cs` lines 530459 to 530513 (class #8292), 543340 to 543405 (the chart class #8538), 540573 to 540581 (class #8500) and 551830 to 551869 (the track class #8661); `stringliteral.json` for `coda` and `[coda]`. Byte scans for callers of 0x20FB9A0, 0x215DA70, 0x215F610, and for loads of the fill-list enumerator `List<#8292>.GetEnumerator` (0x3CCD3C8) and the strings `coda` (0x3CC9410) and `[coda]` (0x3D00770).
- Found: the byte is the written fill's one own field, a `bool` (`dump.cs` line 530462). The fill initialiser stores its third argument there (`fill_init_20FB9A0.asm` line 4). Both chart readers pass 0: the `.mid` reader at `mid_drum_reader_2155050.asm` line 159 and the `.chart` reader at `spec_213e0c0.asm` line 109. The two stream readers pass back a saved byte (`fill_cache_caller_20E49D0.asm` lines 90 to 92). Only one other place writes it. The track finish `0x215C320` first calls `0x215DA70` (`f215c320.asm` line 37), and that method sets the byte to "fill start >= coda tick" for every fill on the track (`fill_skip_from_coda_215DA70.asm` lines 46 to 55, `setge` at line 54) when the chart holds a coda event at `+0x100` (lines 20 to 24; with none it returns and the bytes stay 0). The `.mid` events reader puts the first `[coda]` or `coda` text event there and ignores later ones (`mid_events_coda_21544E0.asm` lines 245 to 312); the `.chart` reader `0x2151650` has the same null test and store at 0x21523A7 to 0x21523E0 (scratch listing). The landing step passes over a fill whose byte is nonzero (`fill_to_activation_20CFF60.asm` lines 86 and 87). So in build 6142 a written fill that starts on or after the first coda event never becomes an activation; this is 1.0's coda skip (FL-39), widened from "on the coda tick" to "at or after it". A mirror method `0x215F610` marks fills before the coda instead (`setl`, line 54) and has no direct caller. Two things were not read: which runs first in a `.mid` load, the events track or the drum track's finish (if the finish ran first, the coda would not yet be set), and where the `.chart` path calls `0x215C320`.
- Proposed label: Proven in the game's code, for what the byte means and who writes it. The listings show every writer the byte scan found and the one reader. The load order caveat above is open, and the block's Evidence line should carry it.
- Hydra: DIFFERS. Hydra's written fill has no such field (`AuthoredFill` in src/parse/song.cpp lines 333 to 336), and a case-insensitive grep of `src/` for `coda` finds no chart rule. Example: a `.mid` drum chart with a `[coda]` text event at tick 400000 and a written fill from 400000 to 400480 that ends on a chord. In the game the fill is skipped and the chord is no activation point; in Hydra `place_authored_fills` (src/parse/song.cpp line 362) lands it and the chord is one. A second example from the other end: a fill that ends one tick before the coda lands in both. This is the 1.1 side of Q-40 (FL-39), which asks about 1.0 only.

## FL-10

- Unknown was: what the game does when two written fills pick one chord; Hydra keeps only the later fill's length.
- Looked at: `docs/clone-hero/code/6142/fill_to_activation_20CFF60.asm` lines 78 to 147 and `fill_deadline_20DDB70.asm` lines 24 to 59; `src/parse/song.cpp` lines 341 to 345 and 355 to 388.
- Found: the landing step makes one record per fill and never looks for an earlier record on the same note. For each fill it finds the chord (line 96), picks the note (line 102), ORs 0x800 into that note's flags (line 106), and builds a new record holding the note at `+0x20` (line 129), the fill start at `+0x28` (line 134), the length at `+0x30` (lines 135 to 138) and active = 1 at `+0x38` (line 139), then appends it (line 145). So two fills that land on one chord give two records that point at the same note, in fill-list order. The deadline loop takes records in list order from a carried index (lines 28 to 45) and stops at the first one whose start is past current + lead (line 45). For each record due it clears that record's active byte and the note's 0x800 flag (lines 47 to 51). The flag is shared, so the earlier-starting fill's deadline clears the note's flag even though the later fill's record is still active. The later record clears it again when its own deadline comes.
- Proposed label: Proven in the game's code. Both records are kept, and the note loses its activation flag at the earlier fill's deadline. Which flag the activation reads, the note's 0x800 or the record's active byte, is outside this block; FL-31's miss handler reads the note's flag through `0x214BB50`.
- Hydra: DIFFERS. `place_authored_fills` calls `apply_fill_end` for each fill (src/parse/song.cpp line 387), which overwrites `activation_length` with chord tick minus the later fill's start (lines 343 and 344), so Hydra's deadline is the later fill's, and the later fill is closer to the chord. Example: fills A from 9600 to 10560 and B from 10080 to 10560 at resolution 480, both landing on a chord at 10560. The game clears the flag when the deadline reaches A's start, 9600 (current tick 7680 with the 1920-tick lead). Hydra prices it at B's start, 10080 (current tick 8160). Between those two points the game says no activation and Hydra says yes. From the other end: when the two fills start on the same tick, both sides agree.

## FL-29

- Unknown was: what the `.mid` reader does with pitch 120 across the four difficulties, and whether Hydra's `.mid` parser hands every difficulty the same fills.
- Looked at: `docs/clone-hero/code/6142/mid_drum_reader_2155050.asm` lines 80 to 101, 151 to 164 and 441 to 557; new listing `track_slice_2152B20.asm` with its `.md`; `track_lookup_20CFD40.asm` lines 31 to 44; `dump.cs` lines 551830 to 551869 (track fields) and 550049 to 550058 (the difficulty enum); `src/parse/song.cpp` lines 759 to 761, 793 to 800, 891 and 912 to 914 and 940 to 942, and 1913 to 1922.
- Found: the `.mid` drum reader asks for the Expert track first (`mid_drum_reader_2155050.asm` lines 90 to 95: `0x20CFD40` with the instrument and difficulty 3, kept in `r13`; Expert is 3 in `dump.cs` line 550057). Pitch 120 adds the fill to that Expert track only (lines 154 to 163). After the event loop, `0x2152B20` returns the instrument's tracks for every difficulty (`track_slice_2152B20.asm` lines 27 to 69), and for each track other than Expert the reader copies Expert's lists into it with `AddRange` (lines 469 to 490). Line 488 is `List<#8292>.AddRange` on `+0x88`, the fill list. The same fill objects end up in all four difficulties' lists; only the `+0x80` list is filtered by difficulty (lines 513 to 515). So a `.mid` fill counts on every difficulty, and FL-27's "one written fill stops generation" holds on all four.
- Proposed label: Proven in the game's code.
- Hydra: same rule. `midi_note_is_read` reads pitch 120 on and off for whatever difficulty is being parsed (src/parse/song.cpp lines 759 to 761 and 912 to 914, 940 to 942), and each difficulty is parsed from the same file (lines 1913 to 1922), so every difficulty records the same fill spans (lines 797 and 798). Not compared: the game shares one fill object across difficulties, so FL-08's skip byte, set by each track's finish, is shared too; Hydra has no such byte (see FL-08).
