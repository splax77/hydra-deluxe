# Player refinements: Hydra's top score on Bomber and Mouths, automatically

Written 2026-11-07, session 490d6781, task refine-plan. Nothing here is built or run before the user's yes.

## The plain version

On 2026-11-07 the auto-player reached Hydra's top score on one chart, Senri, in an automatic run (1,229,730, `C:\Users\Patrick\Downloads\Hydra\hydra-data\ch-mcp-runs\2026-11-07\ls-o3-run1\replies.txt`). The user asked whether the method holds on charts that need more, and chose two: Bomber (Hydra's top path 539,075, path "0- 0 0+ 2 3") and Mouths Like Sidewinder Missiles (552,770, path "1+ 0- 2 0+"). Plain Drums dynamics are out of scope by the user's words: "except for the regular drums, that is an intentional deviation from the game. we dont need to test that".

The player today presses every chord 2 ms early, and a chord on a Star Power end 15 ms early. That is all it knows about timing. These two charts need four more things, and the score files show nothing else the player cannot do.

1. **Squeeze-ins on the end.** Mouths has two phrases whose last chord sits exactly on the Star Power end (chords 431 and 1240, offset 0.0 ms). Hydra collects them inside Star Power, which pushes the end two measures later. The player must press them early, the way it presses an end chord, or the game may pay them outside and never extend. Today it presses them 2 ms early, because it only knows the final end tick, not the nominal one.
2. **Squeeze-outs.** Mouths chord 740 and Bomber chord 180 are phrases Hydra wants hit after Star Power has ended, so the phrase is kept for a later activation. The player must press them late, after the end. Today it presses chord 740 15 ms early (its tick is the end tick), which would collect the phrase instead and change the whole path. Hydra also splits a squeezed-out chord: under its FirstNote rule only the lowest-value gem loses its doubling, so for chord 740 (kick, yellow cymbal, green cymbal) the two cymbals are hit inside and the kick after the end. That is two presses for one chord. Bomber's squeeze-out sits 83.33 ms before the end, further than the game's hit window on that note reaches (about 53 ms), so Bomber also needs the activation pressed early, which moves the end earlier by the same amount.
3. **Fills that end on a kick alone.** Mouths chord 221 and Bomber chord 854 are fills the path passes over whose chord is only a kick. The `kick_only` decline mode would press that kick, and on a kick-only chord the kick is the note that carries the activation flag in Hydra's rule (the game's pick is row P22, unchecked), so the run could activate where Hydra does not. Such a chord must send nothing and let the game's grant hit it.
4. **End chords close to the chord before.** Mouths chord 465 (a kick, on the end of the first activation) is 49.71 ms after chord 464 (a green pad). The load-time check refuses a 15 ms lead there, because it does not look at lanes and models the hit window as a clamped half-gap. The game matches a press to a note on the same pad, and its window for chord 465 is about 62 ms a side. With both facts in the check, 15 ms is allowed and no new number is needed.

Every new number here is the user's to set (questions 2 and 3). Every rule change is a question too (4, 5, 6). The plan runs Mouths first, because it needs only the 0 ms squeezes, then Bomber, which also needs the early activation. Each chart is one launch of about five minutes.

## Prior art

So the timing rules below come from Hydra's model and the game's code, not from prior art.

## The design, point by point

### 1. One place works out when each gem is pressed

Today a chord has one time and one lead. The new rule gives each press its time from the dump's squeeze data, in one function in `play_chart.py` (call it `press_plan`), and `play_chords` fires presses in time order. The loader needs the dump for this, so `load_dump_chords` takes the dump path and path index beside the score path (the same two arguments `declined_chord_indexes` already takes), and the squeeze data for the path comes from the dump's `activations`: `deact_tick`, `nominal_deact_tick`, `sqin_ticks`, `sqout_tick` and `sqinouts`. The rules, with E the Star Power end's time for that activation (the chord's time minus the SqOut's or SqIn's `offset_ms`, since the offset is note minus end):

- A plain chord: its time minus `PRESS_LEAD_MS`. Unchanged.
- A chord on an activation's `deact_tick` whose activation has no SqOut: its time minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`. Unchanged (Senri's 8 end chords).
- A chord on a `deact_tick` whose activation has a SqOut at another tick (Bomber chord 181): plain. Hydra pays it outside (`in_sp` false in the file, SP-41), so an early press buys nothing.
- A SqIn chord: the earlier of (its time minus `PRESS_LEAD_MS`) and (E minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`). Bomber chord 448 at -83.33 ms gets its plain 2 ms; Mouths chords 431 and 1240 at 0.0 ms get 15 ms, the same lead the end chords use. A SqIn after the end (positive offset) gets more; none exists on these charts.
- A SqOut chord: the gem Hydra's FirstNote rule takes the doubling from (note 0 of `Chord::note_list(true)`: the lowest-value gem, ties in kick-red-yellow-blue-green order) is pressed at E plus `SP_OUT_LAG_MS` (question 2). The other gems, if any, are pressed at E minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`, the end-chord rule, because Hydra pays them inside. Mouths chord 740 becomes two presses: yellow and green cymbals at 118,410.95 ms, the kick at 118,440.95 ms. Bomber chord 180 is one red gem, so one late press.
- An activation chord whose SqOut cannot be reached by the lag alone (the late press would fall past the SqOut chord's late window, design point 3) is pressed `SQOUT_ACT_LEAD_MS` early (question 3), and E for that activation moves earlier by the same amount (r = 1; design point 4). Bomber chord 147 is the one case on these charts.

Which gem is note 0 the Python must not work out a second time. `tools/replay.cpp` line 439 writes each chord's `notes` from `ReplayChord::notes`, which `src/core/replay.cpp` lines 140 to 157 fill by walking `ts.chord.note_list(true)` in order (row P30), the same list `src/core/scoring.cpp` line 29 prices with note 0 first. So the squeezed-out gem is the file's first gem, and T1 says so in a comment pointing at `Chord::note_list`. (The fill is skipped when `options.scores_only` is set; `hydra_replay score` does not set it, and the refine-prep files do hold `notes`.) No C++ task is needed.

The press row keeps `lead_ms` as today (negative for a late press) and gains nothing else; a split chord writes two rows with the same `index`. `RunResult.sent` counts presses, so on Mouths it is the 1,321 chords less the 3 declined plus the one extra press of the split chord, and the reply lines of design point 7 explain it.

### 2. Why the squeeze-out needs a lag, and why it is a question

Star Power ends in the first frame whose clock is at or past the end (SP-28). A press that lands in that frame may be scored before or after the check (SP-29, Unknown). A press 15 ms after the end lands about five frames later at 330 fps, so the check has run. The Senri run proved 15 ms on the early side (SP-33's evidence); this plan mirrors it on the late side and recommends 15, but the number is new, so it is question 2. The press must still be inside the chord's late window: 85.72 ms for Mouths chord 740, so 15 ms leaves 70 ms; and it must not reach the next chord on the same pad, which for 740 is 9.8 s away.

Pressing the activation chord 2 ms early moves that end about 2 ms earlier (design point 4), so the real margin after the end is about 17 ms, which only helps.

### 3. Bomber's squeeze-out needs the activation early, and the real window

Bomber chord 180 (one red gem, 32,583.33 ms) sits 83.33 ms before the end at 32,666.67 ms. Its neighbours are 83.33 ms away on both sides, so the game's window on it is 53.06 ms a side (TM-19 to TM-21, worked in "What was checked"). A press 83.33 ms late plus the lag is outside it, so the end must move. Pressing the activation chord 147 (kick and accented yellow cymbal, 27,333.33 ms) early by `SQOUT_ACT_LEAD_MS` moves the end earlier by the same amount at this chart's constant tempo (SP-19, SP-27; r = 1). With 55 ms (question 3's recommendation) the end is 32,611.67 ms; chord 180's red gem is pressed at the end plus 15, which is 32,626.67 ms, 43.33 ms after its note and 9.7 ms inside the 53.06 ms window. Chord 147's own early side is 73.58 ms, so 55 is 18.6 ms inside it, and the chord before it, 146 (a red ghost 83.33 ms earlier), shares no pad with 147. Chord 179 (red, 32,500.00 ms, paid inside by Hydra) stays inside the moved end by 111 ms. Chord 181 (kick and blue cymbal, on the old end) is already outside in Hydra's path. So the moved end costs nothing Hydra counts.

Why 55 and not less: with 40 the late press is 58.33 ms after chord 180, outside its window; with 50 it is 48.33, 4.7 ms inside, under two frames of margin. Why not more: 55 already leaves 18.6 ms on chord 147's early side and the press lands within about 3 ms of its target (Senri: worst press 1.1 ms off apart from the leads). The user may pick another number; the loader's check (design point 5) says whether it loads.

Bomber's squeeze is therefore the one place this plan asks the game for four things nobody has shown: that the stored per-note window is what the hit check enforces (TM-27, Unknown), that it is the same on the early and the late side (TM-26, Unknown; the plan uses one side for both, row P33), that the early activation moves the end by the press's lead (code says so; no run has measured it), and that a red press 43 ms late is matched to chord 180 (the press-handler reading). Each is a row in the Premise map, and the Bomber live step reads each one: the watch's `sp_end_s` after chord 147, and chord 180's row (`hit` 1 and its step of 200). Which rows the run disproves, if it fails, is in the step's fail lines; none of them changes Hydra or the target on its own.

### 4. r is 1, and anything else is out of scope

SP-39 says the end moves by r times the activation's timing error, where r is the end measure's length over the activation measure's. Bomber's `[SyncTrack]` is `0 = TS 6`, `0 = B 180000` and `1152 = TS 4`, and every activation and end is after tick 1152, so r is 1. The loader applies the activation lead with r = 1 and no tempo map. A chart with a tempo or time-signature change between an activation and its squeeze-out end is out of scope (item 1); the loader cannot see the tempo map in the score file, so this plan does not pretend it checks it. Mouths changes tempo often (160 tempo lines), but its squeeze-out needs no activation lead, so r is never used there.

### 5. The load-time check reads lanes and the game's window

`lead_limit_ms` models a note's reach as its half-gap clamped to 37.5 to 85 ms, and checks every end chord against the chord before it whatever pads they hold. Both halves are too strict for these charts. The game's reach is half the formula of the two clamped half-gaps (TM-19 to TM-21), from 48.15 ms a side when both neighbours are within 75 ms to 85.72 ms when both are 170 ms or more away. And a press is matched to a note on its pad (the press handler, FINDINGS lines 56 and 60), so a kick press 15 ms before Mouths chord 465 cannot be taken by chord 464's green pad.

T2 replaces the check with: for every press that is not a plain chord's `PRESS_LEAD_MS` press (an end press, a squeeze press, a late press or an activation lead), the press must fall inside its own gem's window on that side, and must not fall inside the window of the nearest other note on the same pad, before or after. Plain presses stay unchecked, as today, because a 2 ms press has to work on any chart and a real chart holds same-pad notes closer than a window. The window is the game's formula, with the constants as TM-21 writes them, given one home in `tools/ch_probe/constants.py` beside the `RVA_FORMULA_*` tables (values, not addresses; TM-28's naming swap is Q-34 and is not touched). With that, Mouths 465 and 431 load at 15 ms (sides 61.87 and 60.99; no shared pad with 464 or 430), Bomber 147 loads at 55 (side 73.58; chord 146 is red, 147 is kick and yellow cymbal), Bomber 180 loads at 43.33 late (side 53.06; the next red is chord 182, 416.67 ms on), and Senri's chord 2320 (red, 68.0 ms after red chord 2319) still passes: 2319's late side is 48.15 and the press lands 53.0 after it. A lead the check refuses is still refused at load with the chord named, as today.

This changes a rule a passing run used, so it is question 4. It does not touch Hydra's own fixed 85 ms (Q-32 stays open); it is the player's model of what the game accepts.

### 6. Declining a kick-only fill

Today `_kept_gems` keeps at most one gem, the first kick, whatever else the chord holds (`kick_only`). That is wrong on two of these charts' skipped fills. Mouths chord 221 is a kick alone, where the kick is the flagged note and pressing it would activate. Mouths chord 801 holds a kick, a yellow tom and a green tom: sending only the kick leaves the yellow tom unpressed, so it is missed and the combo breaks, which no run can survive on the way to 552,770 (Senri's chord 405, kick and green cymbal, had only the flagged gem left over, which is why one gem was enough there). The new rule is a second decline mode, `all_but_top_pad` (the existing `kick_only` stays as it is, so Senri's passing run is unchanged): send every gem of the chord except the one the game flags, which FL-09 reads as the highest pad (green, then blue, yellow, red); a chord with no pad gem sends nothing, so the game grants the flagged kick (FL-31) and does not activate. On Senri 405 it sends the kick, as `kick_only` did. On Mouths 801 it sends the kick and the yellow tom and leaves the green tom to the grant. Hydra's own rule says the kick is a kick-only chord's activation note (`Chord::activation_note`); the game's picker `0x20D51D0` is read only as "highest pad", and what it does with no pad, and whether "highest" matches Hydra's green-blue-yellow-red order, are row P22, unchecked, settled by Mouths chords 221 and 801 in LS-R1b. The reply line names each declined chord and says what it sent. Question 5.

The press handler's other route (a press on a wrong pad while a fill-end note is in its window hits that note without activating, FINDINGS line 52) is the fallback named in the fail line, not built.

### 7. What the reply says

`ch_run_chart` and `ch_play_chart` gain one line after the declined line: `squeezes: chord 431 in (15 ms before the end), chord 740 out (yellow cymbal, green cymbal 15 ms before the end; kick 15 ms after), chord 1240 in (...)`, and for Bomber `activation chord 147 pressed 55 ms early for the squeeze-out at chord 180`. A score file whose `activations` holds a `sqout_tick` with no `dump_json` is refused naming `dump_json`, because the timing needs the dump. `decline_refusal` already refuses a mode with no dump.

### 8. The machine

`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-ch-run-timing.md` showed two builds during a song starved the game (Run B, 101 of 142) while a quiet machine passed. `ch_start`'s quiet check looks at launch only. A run with a compiler in the song is reported as "not a held machine", as the Senri record was, counts against the step's cap, and its result is read anyway; the user decides whether it counts.
