## The plain version

The auto-player now matches Hydra's top score on two real charts: Mouths (552,770, `C:\Users\Patrick\Downloads\Hydra\hydra-data\ch-mcp-runs\2026-11-07\ls-r1-run2\replies.txt` line 8) and Bomber (539,075, `...\ls-r2ab-run1\replies.txt` line 7). It gets there with rules that only hold on charts like those two. This plan removes each of those limits, one by one, and names the charts and live runs that prove each removal.

The biggest change is how the player handles an activation that has to be pressed early or late so a squeeze works. Today it presses the activation a fixed 55 ms early, assumes the Star Power end moves by the same 55 ms, and refuses any chart where that is not enough or where the end would move by a different amount. After this plan, the player works out the lead each activation needs from the windows of the chords involved, asks Hydra (not its own tempo reader) how far the end moves per millisecond of lead, and then, once the activation has gone in, reads the end the game actually stored and times the squeeze presses from that. Bomber showed why the last step matters: a 53.78 ms early press moved the end by 50.8 ms, because the game stamps the activation with the clock of the frame that read the press, not the clock the player saw (`ls-r2ab-run1\sp_watch-2.csv`: the active byte flips on the row at clock 27.2826 s, press row for chord 147 at 27.2796 s).

The other limits are smaller. The 8 ms gap between a split chord's two presses came out as 12.1 ms in every run, because the Python waits for the press reply and reads the score between the two presses; the fix is to send both groups in one message and let the kit agent, inside the game, make the second write in the first frame at or after 8 ms. A split chord that is also a squeeze-out or a declined fill gets a timing rule instead of a refusal. A squeeze-in after the end gets the mirror of the squeeze-out rule: press the activation late. The press log gets a column for points scored between presses, so a granted fill's points stop showing up on the next chord's row. The main session gets a script for making the dump and score files. And two things become questions for the user because no code change settles them alone: kick dynamics (the game does read a kick velocity, but the kit's kick is a button) and the quiet check's 15 percent.

## The design, point by point

### 1. Hydra owns how far the end moves; the player reads the game's end after the fact

Two owners, one question each. Hydra's C++ answers "if the activation is hit L ms early or late, where does the end go, before the game has run". The game answers "where did the end go", and the player reads it.

The dump gains, per activation, the two ratios `transfer_scale_between` already computes for the display (`src/core/squeeze_rating.cpp` lines 10 to 20): `end_scale_early` and `end_scale_late`, the end's shift per millisecond of early or late activation. It also gains four spans in ms that say how far the ratios stay exact: `steady_ms_before_act`, `steady_ms_after_act`, `steady_ms_before_end`, `steady_ms_after_end`, each the distance from the tick to the nearest tempo or time-signature change on that side, through `SongTiming`'s own tempo map. A lead L early is exact when L is within `steady_ms_before_act` and L times `end_scale_early` within `steady_ms_before_end`; a late lead the same with the after spans and the late ratio. The Python never reads a `[SyncTrack]`. A dump without these fields loads only paths that need no activation lead, and refuses the others naming the field and `hydra_replay dump`, so the old prep files still work for charts like Mouths.

Live, after the first group of an activation chord's press has been sent and its reply has come back, the player reads the engine's Star Power end (`OFF_SP_END_TIME`, the field the watch already logs as `sp_end_s`) and its active byte, through a new `EngineModel.sp_end()` beside `score()`. If the byte is set, every squeeze press of that activation is re-timed from the read end: a squeeze-out's late gem at read end plus `SP_OUT_LAG_MS`, its inside gems and any end-side chord at read end minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`. If the byte is clear (the activation did not fire), the planned times stand and the row says so. The reply gets one line per such activation: "activation chord 820: end planned 186,349.0 ms, game 186,352.1 ms (3.1 later)". `press_plan` stays the one place that works out planned times; the live adjustment is one function, `retime_from_end`, that takes the plan and the read end and returns the moved presses, so the fake-clock tests can drive it.

### 2. The lead is solved per activation from what its squeezes need

Today one constant serves every far squeeze-out. The new rule, in `press_plan`: for each activation with squeezes, collect what each squeeze needs of the activation's timing. A squeeze-out chord needs the activation early by at least `need_out`, which is (end plus `SP_OUT_LAG_MS` minus the chord's time minus its late side), when that is positive. A squeeze-in after the end needs the activation late by at least `need_in`, which is (the chord's time plus `SP_END_LEAD_MS` minus the end minus the chord's early side), when positive. Each need is divided by the matching `end_scale` so it is in the activation's own milliseconds, and `ACT_LEAD_MARGIN_MS` (question 1) is added. The activation's lead is then the smallest number that meets every need. It must fit: within the activation chord's own side on that direction less the same margin, within the steady spans of design point 1, and not inside the window of a same-pad neighbour (the existing lane check). An activation with a need in both directions, or a need that does not fit, is refused at load naming the chord and the milliseconds that do not fit, in plain words ("chord 541 needs the activation 371 ms early; its window is 78 ms"). `SQOUT_ACT_LEAD_MS` goes away, and the squeezes line names the computed lead.

With 6 ms as the margin, worked from the files in `limits-prep` and the chart files (read 2026-11-07; the early ratio at each activation from the tempo just before its tick and just before the end's tick, 4/4 throughout): Rescued chord 820 has need 53.06 (100 plus 15 minus 931's side 61.94), ratio 1.0045, so the lead is 53.06 over 1.0045 plus 6, which is 58.82 ms, against a side of 76.49. The end then moves 59.09 ms, to 186,348.9, and the press for chord 931 at the end plus 15 lands 55.9 ms after 931, 6.0 inside its side. Rescued chord 530 has need 52.57 (the 48-tick gap to chord 561 is 99.04 ms), ratio 1.0284, so the lead is 57.12 against a side of 75.93. Ghost chord 661 has need 57.91, ratio 1.0133, so the lead is 63.15 against 78.75, and chord 756 is pressed 60.3 ms late, 6.1 inside its 66.34. Bomber chord 147 has need 45.27 (83.33 plus 15 minus the squeeze chord 180's side 53.06), ratio 1.0, so the lead is 51.27 instead of 55. The Bomber fixture's test pins whatever the formula gives and the commit message names the change from 55; the other numbers here are the ones the tests should reproduce, and a test that gets another number says which input differs.

### 3. The chords near a moved end move with it

When the end moves earlier by L, every chord Hydra pays inside Star Power (`in_sp` true in the score file) that sits within `SP_END_LEAD_MS` of the moved end is now at risk of paying outside. Today only the chord on `deact_tick` gets the end lead. New rule: every `in_sp` chord of that activation whose time is later than (moved end minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`) is pressed at (moved end minus `SP_END_LEAD_MS`), checked against its window like an end chord, and refused if it does not fit. Bomber chord 179 sits 111 ms before its moved end, so nothing changes there; the rule exists for charts like Rescued with 319 tempo markers, where the test fixture holds a made-up chord 10 ms before the end.

### 4. The 8 ms gap is made inside the game

The kit press message gains an optional `then` list: later groups, each with its elements and `after_ms`. The agent (`kit.ts`, the press handler at lines 488 to 580) writes the first group as today, and writes each later group in the first frame whose time is at or after the first group's down time plus `after_ms`, holding each group `hold_frames` frames from its own down. The reply gains per-group `down_frame`, `up_frame`, `down_ms`, `up_ms`. `play_chords` sends one message for a split chord, reads the score once after the reply, and writes one row per group as today, with each group's own frame columns; the first row carries `score_before` and the last `score_after`, the middle rows repeat them, and `hit` is judged once from the chord's total step. The 8 ms is still `CHORD_ORDER_GAP_MS`; what changes is where it is measured (question 2). At about 330 frames a second the second write lands 8 to 11 ms after the first, which the Mouths and Bomber fixtures' rows show as `down_ms` differences in the live step's read.

### 5. A split chord that is also a squeeze-out or a declined fill

A declined fill's kept gems are pressed in `_order_groups` order with the plain lead, as any split chord is; the activating gem is not among them, so the groups come from multipliers only. A squeeze-out's inside gems are pressed in multiplier order at the inside time, and its late gem last at the late time. If the score file gives the late gem a lower multiplier than any inside gem, Hydra has assumed an order that no press pattern gives, and the loader refuses naming the chord as a Hydra question for the user, not a player bug. No library chart with either case was found (the 22-chart read above), so T3 proves the rules on made-up fixtures.

### 6. A squeeze-in after the end: press the activation late

Design point 2 already covers it: a positive-offset squeeze-in puts a late need on its activation. Yandere Complex (Sewerslvt; md5 `69e905788e726f05b8499037309392e7`, activation tick 195760, squeeze-in tick 203520, offset +80.6 ms) is the one library candidate within reach: the chord must be pressed 95.6 ms before its own time if the end does not move, which no window allows, so the activation goes late by about 95.6 minus the chord's early side plus 6 ms, divided by `end_scale_late`. Whether that fits its activation chord's late side is known only once the prep script has made its files (LS-L0); the step says what to do if it does not.

### 7. Kick dynamics are a question, not a drop

To send one, the kit's kick must be an axis element, pressed with `pad_axis` like a pad, and the game's drum map must bind that axis to action 4. Whether Rewired's "axis as button" binding is what the game's map holds for the kit's element 22, and whether `GetButtonDown` then fires for an axis write, are unknown and need a probe run (LS-K1a and LS-K1b). A plain or accent kick presses at the top axis value today in effect (a pressed button reads as 1.0, and the helper turns that into velocity 127: row P30, unchecked). ### 8. The window edges and the same-pad matching rule get a probe

A new experiment script, `tools/ch_probe/experiments/edge_probe.py` (the older `walk_edges.py` plays this chart too, but it sends keys to the focused game, which the 2026-10-30 ruling above rules out, and it has no result file, TM-27's Evidence line), wrapped as the tool `ch_edge_probe(song_folder, score_json, probes_json)`: it plays the chart exactly as `ch_play_chart` does, except that the chords `probes_json` names get the lead it gives them (positive early, negative late), unchecked by the loader (the probe is the point) and marked `probe` in a new press-row column so a read cannot mistake them. The chart is `C:\Clone Hero\songs\Hydra Probe\Edge Walk` (its notes are row P27, checked; the main session dumps it with the prep script and picks eight of its isolated notes, each with a side of 85.72, and the executor adds one pair of same-pad notes 100 ms apart to a copy of the chart under `Hydra Probe`, because the chart on disk has no pair). The probes: side minus 3 ms and side plus 3 ms, early and late, one probe on each of the eight notes; and on the pair, a press 60 ms after the first note. Each read is a hit or a miss in the row, and for the pair, which note was taken shows as the next press's step and the stats' combo. The results write TM-26, TM-27 and a new block for the matching rule, and they decide whether the lane check keeps refusing a press inside a later same-pad note's window (Rescued's blocker). Question 3 asks for this tool.

### 9. Points scored between presses get their own column

Before each press the player reads the score fresh. The difference from the previous row's `score_after` goes in a new column `scored_between`, and the row's own step is `score_after` minus that fresh read. The reply and `ch_play_status` attribute each non-zero `scored_between` to the most recent declined chord not yet credited, as "chord 854 granted 200 before chord 855", and name an amount with no pending declined chord as "unexplained". `PRESS_LOG_COLUMNS` grows by that column; `score_compare.py` and `ch_compare` read by name and are checked still to pass on the two kept press logs.

### 10. The inputs come from a script the main session runs

`tools/ch_probe/prep_inputs.py --chart <notes.chart or notes.mid> --out <folder> [--exe <hydra_replay.exe>] [--cap 4] [--ms 10]` runs `hydra_replay dump` and then `score --path <dump> --index 0`, with profile 39's settings as the defaults (cap 4, ms 10, from the README of `limits-prep`; the test pins them), writes `<name>-dump.json` and `<name>-score.json` into the folder, then dry-loads them with `all_but_top_pad` and prints the same lines `ch_run_chart` would print (declined, squeezes, order, activation leads with their planned ends and ratios) or the loader's refusal. It prints the exe's build date too. Agents never run it (it calls `hydra_replay`); the main session does, as LS-L0.
